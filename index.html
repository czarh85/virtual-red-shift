An unofficial Virtual Boy port of VVVVVV · work in progress

Virtual Red Shift

VVVVVV running on real Virtual Boy hardware — 434 rooms, in red and black, on a console from 1995 that was never meant to scroll. It is not finished, and it needs testers.

From the published VVVVVV source code, this port takes the game's logic as a reference and the level data: room layouts, entity placement and text. Every graphic and sound is drawn for the Virtual Boy; none of the original art, music or sound is used.

It is built from the source Terry Cavanagh released in 2020, It is a modified version, not the original game, and not endorsed by its authors.

Status

Nothing is distributed yet. A test build, and the testing programme around it, will be released only once the author of VVVVVV has given permission for it. That request is being prepared.

The 3D

Everything you interact with sits at the screen surface: floor, walls, spikes, platforms, the player. The depth is behind them.

Each room is a box. The backdrop sits further back and shows through its open parts, deeper in some zones than others. When a gravity line flips you, the room draws back a moment, then settles.

A few things come forward: trinkets float out of the screen, and enemies sit just in front. On the title screen, the six letters recede one by one.

Depth changes are gradual and kept comfortable. Two earlier ideas, spikes and enemies pushed further out, were dropped after console testing.

How it is made

One person and an AI coding assistant (Claude), working through written roles, or skills: an architect plans a change, a coder applies it, a tester checks the result. A loop runs the three in sequence, unattended: the port is 412 commits in five days, and the longest run went through a night, 126 commits in eight hours. The roles were written and sharpened on an earlier Virtual Boy game, Virtual Breakout. Nothing reaches the cartridge without the tester's verdict, and a build that fails a check is refused. Several ways of running coders in parallel were tried and timed, to speed up the port.

How bugs will be reported

For when a build is released.

Report the room, not the game

Every room prints a four-character code in the right margin. A report is three lines:

Room F013
Doing walked in from the left, flipped once
Happened fell through the floor

That is enough to find it. Stereoscopic depth cannot be judged on a screenshot: if something hurts your eyes, looks flat or flickers, that goes in the report too. Two depth ideas have already been dropped that way.

Where to send it

Reports go to the issue tracker: github.com/czarh85/virtual-red-shift/issues. The repository holds only this page and exists to receive reports.

One room per issue, with the room code in the title.

If you hold rights in anything shown here and want it changed or removed, open an issue and it will be done.

The soundtrack

This project does not distribute any VVVVVV music. The soundtrack is Magnus Pålsson's work; a build check rejects any public ROM carrying even a transcription of it.

ESSound streams audio from the cartridge's microSD card alongside the console's own sound, so a player who owns the soundtrack could point it at their own copy. Nothing is written for it yet and the hardware is not out.

MrCart 128 by Mr Flower: 128 Mbit, with its own audio path.
